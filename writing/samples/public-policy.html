Policy Analysis and Recommendations for the Regional Bus Fleet Replacement Program

Prioritizing Renewal of the Urban Bus Fleet in Osorno Province

Mitchell Langley · 2026

Writing Sample Context. This report was prepared in 2026 as an independent policy analysis of Renueva Tu Micro (“Renew Your Bus”), a publicly funded bus fleet replacement program administered by Chilean regional governments pursuant to national transportation law. It reviews the rules adopted by the Regional Government of Los Lagos for the 2026 funding round, compares those rules with fleet and program data from Osorno Province, and proposes changes for a subsequent round. Chilean regional governments are subnational public authorities with elected regional councils and statutorily defined administrative and budgetary powers. This writing sample is an English translation of the original Spanish-language work product. The underlying analysis, findings, and recommendations have not been changed.

Executive Summary

This report reviews the rules governing the 2026 Renueva Tu Micro bus fleet replacement program, adopted by the Regional Government of Los Lagos through Exempt Resolution No. 1880/2026. The analysis is limited to matters that fall within the authority granted to regional governments by Supreme Decree No. 44/2011 of the Ministry of Transport and Telecommunications (MTT). It does not address the amount of the individual replacement incentives, which MTT establishes nationally and which therefore falls outside the Regional Government's authority.

The point of departure is the urban bus fleet in Osorno, which operates under a Regulated Service Area (Perímetro de Exclusión). For purposes of this translation, “Regulated Service Area” refers to the specific Chilean regulatory mechanism under which MTT establishes operating conditions for public transportation services within a defined geographic area. Osorno is consequently subject to a maximum bus age of 20 years under MTT Resolution No. 3/2021, rather than the general national maximum of 22 years. Forty-seven of Osorno's 246 registered urban buses, or 19.11 percent of the fleet, were manufactured in 2007 or earlier and will therefore reach or exceed the applicable 20-year limit within the horizon of the next funding round. A further 10 buses manufactured in 2008 will reach that limit during 2027, bringing the identified near-term replacement need to 57 vehicles.

The results of the 2026 funding round confirm that this regulatory pressure is not adequately reflected in the existing selection methodology. Osorno received only 1 of the 21 urban awards made during the first stage of the 2026 program. At the same time, 28 of the 37 applicants placed on the regional urban waiting list, or 75.7 percent, were from Osorno. The result is a substantial mismatch between the location of the documented replacement need and the allocation produced by the current selection criteria.

Three changes are proposed for a future funding round. The first is to revise the allocation of resources between the Urban and Rural categories, or alternatively to establish an Osorno-specific suballocation within the Urban category. The second is to revise the first-stage order of priority so that vehicles operating within a Regulated Service Area receive greater weight. The third is to strengthen territorial retention by extending the minimum period during which replacement vehicles must remain within the Los Lagos Region and by creating a selection preference for applicants that voluntarily commit to retaining the replacement vehicle in urban service in Osorno.

Each proposal is limited by the scope of the authority delegated to the Regional Government. Most importantly, the governing regulation allows restrictions on reassignment at the regional level, but does not authorize the Regional Government to impose an additional mandatory restriction at the provincial or municipal level. An Osorno-specific retention measure should therefore be structured as a voluntary selection criterion rather than as a mandatory post-award condition. Any differential treatment in favor of Osorno should also be supported expressly by objective technical evidence, both to satisfy the requirements applicable to reasoned administrative decision-making and to reduce the risk of claims of arbitrary or unequal treatment by operators elsewhere in the Los Lagos Region.

1. Introduction

This report analyzes Exempt Resolution No. 1880/2026 of the Regional Government of Los Lagos, which establishes the rules governing the 2026 Renueva Tu Micro funding round, and proposes changes for a future round. The analysis is confined to the powers that Supreme Decree No. 44/2011 of the Ministry of Transport and Telecommunications, as subsequently amended, grants to regional governments.

The report therefore does not propose changes to the nationally established incentive amounts, including vehicle purchase value and the supplemental incentive components. Those amounts are established annually by MTT and are currently governed by Exempt Resolution No. 741/2026. They are outside the rulemaking authority of the Regional Government.

The report incorporates a quantitative analysis of the age profile of Osorno's urban bus fleet using the National Public Transportation Registry (Registro Nacional de Transporte Público, RNSTP). Those data are compared with the MTT regulations governing maximum vehicle age. The purpose is to ensure that the recommendations that follow are based on objective and verifiable information and that any future decision by the Regional Government can be supported by a documented technical basis.

2. Governing Policy and Regulatory Framework

  • Law No. 20.378 (2010), Fourth Transitional Article. As amended by Law No. 21.692, this provision creates the special funding mechanism used by the program and authorizes regional governments to conduct fleet replacement funding rounds.
  • Supreme Decree No. 44/2011, Ministry of Transport and Telecommunications. This is the principal regulation governing the Special Program for the Replacement of Buses, Minibuses, Trolleybuses, and Taxibuses.
  • Exempt Resolution No. 1880/2026, Regional Government of Los Lagos. This resolution establishes the rules governing the 2026 regional funding round.
  • Regional Council Certificate No. 151, March 20, 2026. This records the Regional Council's approval of the 2026 distribution of program resources, with 50 percent assigned to the Urban category and 50 percent assigned to the Rural category.
  • Exempt Resolution No. 741/2026, MTT. This establishes the vehicle purchase values and incentive amounts applicable during 2026. Those amounts are not analyzed in this report.
  • Decree No. 44 of June 18, 2024, MTT. This establishes a general maximum vehicle age of 22 years for buses and taxibuses providing compensated public transportation service. Services operating in cities, provinces, or municipalities subject to a Regulated Service Area or equivalent mechanism are expressly excluded from that general rule and remain subject to the maximum age established by the resolution governing the particular regulated system.
  • Resolution No. 3/2021, MTT. This establishes the operating conditions applicable to Osorno's urban public transportation Regulated Service Area and fixes a maximum age of 20 years for buses assigned to that system.

3. Regulatory Diagnosis: Age of Osorno's Urban Bus Fleet

Osorno is one of the municipalities in Chile in which urban public transportation operates under a Regulated Service Area. Decree No. 44/2024 identifies Castro, Quellón, Osorno, and Puerto Montt among the locations in the Los Lagos Region with active regulated-area arrangements. In the case of Osorno, MTT Resolution No. 3/2021 establishes a maximum vehicle age of 20 years. The applicable local standard is therefore more stringent than the general 22-year maximum that applies where no Regulated Service Area or equivalent mechanism is in force.

The June 2026 RNSTP vehicle registry makes it possible to quantify the resulting replacement requirement. The next Renueva Tu Micro funding round is expected in 2027. A vehicle manufactured in 2007 will therefore reach 20 years of age during that year. For purposes of the analysis, vehicles manufactured in 2007 or earlier are treated as the group that will reach or exceed the applicable limit within the horizon of the next funding round.

2026 Program Outcomes

The results of the 2026 funding round reflect the same problem from a different direction. According to Regional Public Transit Division Official Letter No. 17189/2026, which reports the results of the first stage and the associated waiting list, Osorno received only one of the 21 urban awards, equivalent to 4.8 percent of the total. The single award, made to operator DTCV15 (Américo Pizarro Queulo), totaled CLP $105,490,000.

At the same time, 28 of the 37 applicants placed on the urban waiting list, or 75.7 percent, were from Osorno. Those applications represented CLP $2,077,930,000 in requested funding awaiting award.

The imbalance is relevant because Osorno was heavily represented on the waiting list while receiving only one initial award, notwithstanding a documented fleet-age exposure of 19.11 percent. The result indicates that the current selection methodology, in which Regulated Service Area status occupies fourth place in the order of priority, does not adequately reflect the particular regulatory pressure affecting Osorno. Vehicles in other cities may therefore receive priority even where the applicable fleet-age constraint is less immediate.

The 2026 results accordingly provide additional support for reconsidering both the allocation of resources within the Urban category and the position assigned to Regulated Service Area status in the first-stage selection criteria.

4. Assessment of the 2026 Program Rules

4.1 Allocation of Funding Between Urban and Rural Categories

The 2026 rules allocate 50 percent of program resources to urban buses and 50 percent to rural buses. Supreme Decree No. 44 does not require this distribution and does not prescribe another fixed percentage. The current allocation is a decision of the Regional Government, approved through Regional Council Certificate No. 151/2026.

The distinction matters because the Regional Government retains authority to reconsider both the percentage allocated to each category and the structure used to distribute those resources in a future funding round. The fleet and program data summarized above provide a technical basis on which that authority may be exercised.

4.2 First-Stage Prioritization Within the Urban Category

Within the Urban category, outgoing vehicles from cities or areas operating under a Regulated Service Area, including Osorno, currently occupy fourth place in the order of priority. They rank below buses replaced because of vandalism, applications proposing incoming electric vehicles, and applications proposing incoming vehicles meeting the Santiago RED fleet standard.

Article 11 of Supreme Decree No. 44 gives regional governments authority to establish the selection criteria applicable to each funding round. It does not impose the current order. The Regional Government may therefore revise that order in a future call for applications.

In Osorno, Regulated Service Area status is directly connected to the fleet-age problem identified above. Resolution No. 3/2021 imposes a 20-year maximum precisely because the buses operate within that regulatory framework. The current placement of the criterion in fourth position does not reflect the urgency created when a significant portion of the local fleet is approaching the maximum age permitted under the resolution.

4.3 Territorial Retention of Replacement Vehicles

Under the 2026 rules, an incoming replacement vehicle may not change its type of service or leave the Los Lagos Region for a minimum period of 36 months beginning with the second stage of the application process. This requirement reproduces Article 15(b) of Supreme Decree No. 44.

Two issues are relevant to a future funding round:

  • Duration. The regulation establishes a minimum period of “at least” 36 months. The Regional Government may therefore establish a longer minimum.
  • Geographic scope. The restriction applies to the vehicle's region and type of service. It does not establish an equivalent restriction at the level of a province or municipality. Under the present framework, a vehicle replaced in Osorno could therefore later be reassigned to urban service elsewhere in the Los Lagos Region without violating Article 15(b).

5. Recommendations for a Future Funding Round

The proposals that follow are limited to powers expressly available to the Regional Government under Supreme Decree No. 44. For each proposal, the relevant authority is identified together with the principal procedural or legal limitation on its use. The Regional Government remains free to adopt, modify, or reject any of the proposals. The quantitative analysis provides a technical basis for that decision; it does not convert a discretionary policy choice into a legal obligation.

Proposal A: Adjust the Allocation of Resources Between Program Categories

Article 2 of Supreme Decree No. 44 provides that annual program expenditures are subject to the maximum amount that each Regional Government determines to allocate to the program. The current 50/50 division between the Urban and Rural categories does not originate in Supreme Decree No. 44. It was established by decision of the Regional Council.

Two alternatives are available and may be used separately or together:

  • Modify the percentage allocated to each category. The Regional Government may assign a larger share of program resources to the Urban category. Within that category, Osorno's Regulated Service Area status and documented concentration of aging vehicles would then operate together with the revised selection criteria proposed below.
  • Establish a geographic suballocation within the Urban category. A defined percentage of Urban-category resources may be reserved for applications involving vehicles operating in Osorno Province.

The available data permit either alternative to be supported by a specific factual record. Osorno has 246 registered urban buses, 47 of which fall within the immediate age group identified in this report, with most of those vehicles concentrated among three operators. A decision based on those figures is more readily defensible than one based on a generalized preference for Osorno, particularly if operators in other provinces question the allocation.

Procedural consideration. A change in the percentage distribution of program resources would require approval by the Regional Council, just as the existing distribution was approved through Regional Council Certificate No. 151/2026. It cannot be adopted solely through an administrative decision of the Regional Government's Infrastructure and Transportation Division.
Basis for a geographic suballocation. If the Regional Government adopts an Osorno-specific suballocation, the resolution should identify the objective evidence supporting that treatment. Relevant considerations include the age profile of Osorno's urban fleet, the 20-year maximum established by Resolution No. 3/2021, the timetable under which additional vehicles will reach that limit, and, if appropriate, comparison with other provincial capitals in the Los Lagos Region. The purpose is to establish a documented technical basis for the distinction and to reduce the risk that the measure could be characterized as arbitrary or unequal.

Proposal B: Restructure First-Stage Urban Selection Priorities

Article 11 of Supreme Decree No. 44 expressly authorizes regional governments to determine the selection criteria applicable to each funding round. It does not prescribe either a fixed number of criteria or a mandatory order of priority. The current hierarchy may therefore be revised.

The proposed change would move Regulated Service Area status from fourth to second place, immediately after buses replaced because of vandalism. The justification is the documented convergence in Osorno of two separate regulatory conditions: operation under a Regulated Service Area and the 20-year maximum vehicle age imposed through Resolution No. 3/2021.

The vehicle-age requirement affects 19.11 percent of Osorno's urban fleet within the planning horizon analyzed here and is concentrated among identifiable operators. Buses replaced because of vandalism address an immediate emergency and public-safety condition, which supports retaining that criterion in first place. Vehicles affected by Osorno's Regulated Service Area face a different form of regulatory urgency because they must leave service upon reaching the applicable maximum age. That condition supports assigning the criterion greater weight than it receives under the current rules.

Article 11 would allow the Regional Government to place Regulated Service Area status either first or second. The choice between those positions is a policy decision rather than a question of legal authority. If the hierarchy is changed, the resolution should state the reason for the change expressly and should identify the fleet-age data on which the decision relies.

Drafting consideration. Any revision should preserve the existing substantive definition of the Regulated Service Area criterion, including its reference to Law No. 18.696 and the continuation of qualifying operating arrangements through an applicable resolution. This avoids narrowing the criterion inadvertently or creating unnecessary uncertainty as to its scope.

Proposal C: Strengthen Territorial Retention of Replacement Vehicles

A two-part mechanism is proposed. The first relies on the authority to establish a longer regional retention period. The second uses the Regional Government's authority over selection criteria to encourage continued operation in Osorno without imposing a restriction that exceeds the geographic scope authorized by the national regulation.

1. Extend the Minimum Regional Retention Period

Article 15(b) establishes a minimum retention period of “at least” 36 months. The Regional Government may therefore establish a longer period, such as 48 or 60 months, for the next funding round. Extending the period would provide greater assurance that vehicles renewed with regional resources remain within the Los Lagos Region for a meaningful period after the public investment is made.

2. Incentivize Voluntary Retention in Osorno

Article 15(b) restricts changes in the vehicle's type of service and region. It does not authorize a corresponding mandatory restriction at the provincial or municipal level. A condition requiring a replacement vehicle to remain exclusively in the city or municipality of Osorno would therefore go beyond the authority delegated to the Regional Government by the regulation.

Supreme Decree No. 44 is a national regulation issued jointly by the Ministries of Transport, Interior, and Finance. A regional funding resolution cannot unilaterally expand the geographic scope of the restriction established in Article 15(b). An Osorno-specific mandatory retention requirement should therefore not be included as a condition of award.

The same policy objective can be pursued through Article 11. A future funding round could assign additional points or priority to applicants that voluntarily commit, through a sworn declaration or contractual undertaking, to retain the replacement vehicle in Osorno urban service for a defined period.

The distinction is material. A mandatory restriction imposed after award must remain within the limits established by Article 15. A voluntary commitment used as a selection criterion falls instead within the broader authority granted by Article 11 to determine how applications are prioritized.

6. Cross-Cutting Implementation Considerations

The following considerations apply across the three proposals and should be reflected in the design of any future funding round.

Distinguish Discretionary Powers from Rule-Bound Requirements

Supreme Decree No. 44 does not grant the same degree of discretion in all matters. The allocation of resources under Article 2 and the definition of selection criteria under Article 11 leave substantial room for regional policy choices. Eligibility requirements and post-award restrictions under Articles 4, 5, and 15 are more closely defined by the regulation itself.

Those more closely defined provisions may be adjusted only where the regulation expressly permits it. The clearest example is the authority to extend a stated minimum retention period. They should not be used to create new categories of mandatory restriction, such as municipal-level vehicle retention, where the national regulation does not provide for them.

Establish an Objective Technical Basis for Geographic Differentiation

Any decision that gives Osorno different treatment from other provinces in the Los Lagos Region should be supported by objective evidence. The quantitative analysis in this report provides such a basis. Osorno has 246 registered urban buses, 47 of which fall within the immediate age group identified here. Those vehicles are concentrated among identifiable operators, and their regulatory significance derives from the 20-year maximum established specifically for the Osorno system by Resolution No. 3/2021.

Those facts should be incorporated expressly into the administrative record supporting any geographic allocation or change in selection criteria. Doing so would provide the technical foundation required for a reasoned administrative decision and would help address potential claims that the Regional Government had treated operators in different parts of the region arbitrarily or without sufficient justification.

Use the Appropriate Decision-Making Procedure

The proposals also require different procedural routes. A change in the percentage distribution of program resources requires Regional Council approval. Changes to the selection criteria may be established administratively when the funding-round rules are adopted pursuant to Article 11. An extension of the regional retention period may likewise be established in the funding-round rules pursuant to Article 15(b).

7. Conclusion

The Regional Government of Los Lagos has authority to modify several elements of the next Renueva Tu Micro funding round in response to the replacement needs documented in Osorno. The available evidence supports consideration of a revised allocation between the Urban and Rural categories, an Osorno-specific suballocation within the Urban category, greater priority for vehicles operating under a Regulated Service Area, and a longer regional retention period combined with an incentive for voluntary continued operation in Osorno.

That authority is subject to identifiable limits. Mandatory post-award restrictions must remain within the geographic scope established by Supreme Decree No. 44. Any differential treatment of Osorno should also be supported by objective evidence and by an express statement of the technical considerations that justify the distinction.

The decision to adopt, modify, or reject the proposals remains a decision of regional public policy. Whichever approach is adopted, the resolution or other administrative instrument formalizing that decision should identify the relevant fleet-age data and the requirements of MTT Resolution No. 3/2021 as part of its stated technical basis. This would connect the exercise of the Regional Government's discretion to the documented conditions of the Osorno fleet and provide a clearer basis for implementation and subsequent administrative review.
