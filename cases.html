Case studies

What the work actually looks like

Six engagements in Chilean public transport, from subsidy arithmetic to environmental permitting. Each one starts with the source records rather than the summary report.

Engagements Most recent first

  1. Antofagasta, Copiapó, Villarrica
    2026

    Comparative tender design review

    Three generations of electric public transport tender, read side by side: fleet ownership, payment mechanism, indexation, demand risk and the penalties that make or break an operator’s margin.

    A parameter by parameter comparison showing where risk moved from the state to the operator between 2020 and 2025, and what each shift cost.

  2. Osorno
    2025

    Operational subsidy distribution model

    An operator inside an exclusion perimeter was distributing Law 20.378 subsidy among its owners on a rule nobody could reconstruct. Rebuilt the allocation from service records, contracted kilometres and fleet registration.

    Per owner statements that reconcile line by line to the ministry payment file, produced automatically each month.

  3. Coquimbo and La Serena
    2025

    Electric bus charging depot, permitting

    Environmental pertinence enquiry for a charging centre serving the conurbation fleet: land use, connection capacity, noise and the question of whether the project had to enter environmental assessment at all.

    Confirmed outside mandatory assessment, removing an estimated twelve months from the permitting path.

  4. Los Lagos
    2025 to 2026

    Defence in sanction proceedings

    Written defences against charge sheets issued by a regional transport authority, covering service frequency, fleet condition and reporting obligations.

    Charges answered on the record within the statutory deadline, with the evidence reconstructed from the operator’s own systems.

  5. National
    2026

    Fleet renewal outcomes

    Crossed the national transport registry against the beneficiary lists of the bus renewal programme to test whether the subsidy actually lowered fleet age, and where it did not.

    A measured change in average fleet age by region and operator size, with the cases where replacement simply moved older vehicles elsewhere.

  6. Temuco
    2025

    Operational analytics, Line 2

    A dashboard built on the operator’s own GPS and dispatch records: departures actually run against departures contracted, headway gaps, and the vehicles behind them.

    Compliance measured daily instead of argued monthly.
